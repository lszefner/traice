# Snapshot provenance

`articles.json` is a dated metadata snapshot built by `python scripts/collect.py` from the feeds listed in its `feeds` field, plus one manually checked Stanford Report example. It contains titles, dates, URLs, feed-derived topic labels and publisher names, not article bodies. The collection date is stored in the file. It is a demonstration sample, not a representative or exhaustive picture of AI news.

Article links to research are included only when a feed's article content contains a recognizable DOI or arXiv URL and the work was resolved against OpenAlex, or when manually checked for the Stanford example. The Stanford article [directly links](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2025/12/ai-benchmarks-flaws-models-bugs-research) to [arXiv:2511.16842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16842). The graph records the article URL as provenance. Matching a URL does not establish that every claim in a story is supported by that paper. A failed match is not evidence of no paper.

Paper title and citation metadata come from OpenAlex; only citations connecting two already imported papers are retained. Feed topics are category labels, not verified semantic classifications. The collector limits OpenAlex lookups to 30 per run and feeds provide a recent window rather than a historical archive. Refreshing can change both the number of articles and which paper links resolve. Check current source terms, robots rules, feed access and redistribution rights before larger collection or public redistribution; full text is not included. The metadata snapshot itself has not been independently audited for licensing.

The importer merges nodes and relationships by stable identifiers. Re-running it adds or updates the snapshot but does not delete old records or remove stale relationships; use a fresh database for a clean snapshot. Run with `NEO4J_URI`, `NEO4J_USER` and `NEO4J_PASSWORD` set: `python scripts/ingest.py`.

In the included snapshot collected 2026-11-02 UTC: 232 articles (150 ScienceDaily, 81 MIT News, one Stanford Report); 12 articles have at least one resolved explicit link, covering 15 distinct papers. There are zero paper-to-paper citation edges within this small matched set. Each paper entry retains the extracted `linked_url` separately from the canonical OpenAlex paper URL; `feed_urls` records feeds where the article appeared. The Stanford example was manually checked on the article page.

## The Conversation supplement

On 2026-11-02 UTC we appended 25 records from [The Conversation's AI Atom feed](https://theconversation.com/topics/artificial-intelligence-ai-90/articles.atom): four contain links resolving to research works. This produces 257 total records; it does not establish multiple-outlet coverage of the same papers. The Conversation is independently edited, and articles are written by academics; it is not a conventional general-news sample. Author credits and original URLs are retained in the metadata, and each resolved link retains its feed provenance. See [its US republishing guidelines](https://theconversation.com/us/republishing-guidelines): articles are published under an Attribution/No Derivatives licence, subject to guidelines and third-party exceptions. Our metadata-only extraction does not by itself establish rights to republish full stories or to operate a commercial service. No article body or image is included. The feed allowed access under its robots.txt for the collector user agent when checked; access and usage terms can change. Use `python -m scripts.add_conversation` to append new entries to an existing snapshot, rather than replacing the original sample.

To rebuild both portions from current feeds, run `python scripts/collect.py` first, then `python -m scripts.add_conversation`. The first command replaces the base snapshot; the second appends only unseen Conversation URLs. Both fetch live feeds and OpenAlex, so their output and link yield can change and a refresh is not guaranteed to reproduce the pinned snapshot.
